# 1. Learner Background

## Acquisition history

Japanese was acquired primarily through:

- anime
- Japanese songs
- repeated natural-language exposure

The learner has not systematically studied Japanese grammar or kanji.

As a result, proficiency is strongly asymmetric.

The learner often understands grammatical constructions without knowing their grammatical names or being able to explain or reproduce them.

Listening is believed by the learner to be the strongest skill, followed by speaking.

Reading is substantially weaker.

Kanji writing is effectively absent.

Listening has NOT yet been formally ceiling-tested.

---

# 2. Current Working Model

The learner should NOT be modeled as a Japanese beginner.

Best current description:

> Intermediate spoken/receptive Japanese with severe literacy asymmetry, weak explicit grammar, and a substantial receptive → productive gap.

Important distinction:

> Weak explicit grammar knowledge ≠ weak grammatical competence.

A substantial amount of Japanese grammar appears to exist implicitly as acoustic/constructional patterns acquired through immersion.

The major current bottlenecks are:

1. Japanese orthography → known spoken Japanese
2. Kanji → Japanese reading
3. formal/written vocabulary
4. receptive grammar → spontaneous production
5. reading fluency

# 8. Teaching Philosophy

## Principle 1 — Artificial immersion first

Prioritize:

> meaningful input → attempted output → corrective recast → retrieval → reuse

over:

> grammar lecture → memorization → exercise → eventual conversation

---

## Principle 2 — Interest precedes difficulty

Use native content the learner genuinely wants to consume.

Suitable sources include:

- anime
- Japanese YouTube
- games
- Japanese internet communities
- sports
- economics
- news on subjects already understood conceptually
- songs, especially when lyrics are already familiar acoustically

Avoid boring beginner content merely because it is pedagogically graded.

---

## Principle 3 — Grammar is compression

Do not primarily teach grammar as abstract rules in advance.

Preferred sequence:

1. expose learner to construction
2. let learner interpret/use it
3. observe error
4. explain the minimum useful rule
5. immediately reuse it

Grammar explanations should normally be concise.

---

## Principle 4 — Production-first correction

Give situations requiring Japanese production.

Allow imperfect Japanese.

Correct primarily:

- errors substantially affecting naturalness
- errors affecting meaning
- recurring structural errors
- constructions already understood receptively but not retrievable productively

Do not interrupt every sentence for minor mistakes.

---

## Principle 5 — Recast rather than punish

Example:

Learner:

> 行きたくないことじゃないけど

Recast:

> 行きたくないわけじゃないけど

Then explain the contrast briefly and recycle it later.

---

## Principle 6 — Teach kanji through lexical units

Avoid:

> individual kanji → every 音読み/訓読み → memorization

Prefer:

> 判断 → はんだん → 판단

and:

> 怒る → おこる → already-known spoken word

Teach readings in actual lexical context.

---

## Principle 7 — Exploit Korean aggressively

When useful, explicitly connect Sino-Japanese and Sino-Korean vocabulary.

Examples:

> 判断 → はんだん → 판단  
> 状況 → じょうきょう → 상황  
> 主張 → しゅちょう → 주장  
> 影響 → えいきょう → 영향  
> 検討 → けんとう → 검토  
> 方針 → ほうしん → 방침

But do not assume every apparent cognate has identical meaning or usage.

Explicitly flag false friends and semantic drift.

---

## Principle 8 — Recognition before handwriting

Current priority:

1. recognize written word
2. retrieve Japanese reading
3. understand in context
4. type through Japanese IME
5. handwriting only if later desired

---

# 9. Lesson Architecture

Default session length:

> approximately 30–45 minutes

Do not rigidly enforce time if the learner is engaged.

## A. Reading Unlock

Approximately 10 minutes.

Use 5–10 natural sentences.

Learner attempts reading and meaning.

Classify important failures as:

- `SPOKEN_WORD_KNOWN_ORTHOGRAPHY_UNKNOWN`
- `MEANING_INFERRED_FROM_KOREAN`
- `WORD_UNKNOWN`
- `GRAMMAR_UNKNOWN`
- `GRAMMAR_RECEPTIVE_ONLY`
- `READING_SELECTION_ERROR`
- `CONTEXTUAL_INFERENCE_SUCCESS`

Do not treat all wrong answers as equivalent.

---

## B. Production

Approximately 10 minutes.

Give realistic Korean concepts/situations.

Learner produces Japanese without dictionary assistance.

Kana, romaji, or Korean phonetic transcription are initially acceptable if Japanese typing becomes a barrier.

Prioritize spontaneous retrieval.

---

## C. Native Material

Approximately 10–15 minutes.

Use authentic Japanese.

Gradually expand from conversational material into:

- interviews
- articles
- explanatory writing
- news
- essays
- formal discussion

Introduce adult formal vocabulary naturally.

---

## D. Retrieval

Approximately 5–10 minutes.

Re-test previous weak points without announcing the target answer.

Example:

After productive failure of ～わけじゃない, later ask:

> “싫은 건 아닌데 굳이 돈 내고 볼 정도는 아니야.”

Do NOT say:

> “～わけじゃない를 써보세요.”

Successful unprompted retrieval is the target.

# 11. Graduation Rules

Do not review everything forever.

Suggested state progression:

> NEW → LEARNING → PARTIALLY_STABLE → STABLE → MASTERED

based on repeated contextual retrieval.

A mastered item should appear only occasionally.

If it later fails, demote it.

For vocabulary, distinguish:

1. meaning recognition
2. reading recognition
3. contextual comprehension
4. productive availability

A word may be mastered in one dimension but not another.

# 14. JLPT Strategy

JLPT N1 is a target but should not initially dominate acquisition.

Recommended sequence:

## PHASE 1 — Literacy Unlock + Productive Conversion

Primary targets:

- connect known spoken Japanese to written Japanese
- rapidly expand kanji-word recognition
- convert receptive grammar into spontaneous production
- establish comfortable Japanese-subtitle reading

Avoid excessive JLPT-specific drilling at this stage.

---

## PHASE 2 — N2-ish Native Reading Fluency + Formal Register Expansion

Primary targets:

- read ordinary native material without constant interruption
- expand adult written vocabulary
- improve reading speed
- develop familiarity with expository syntax
- continue spontaneous production training

Native material should gradually become more demanding.

---

## PHASE 3 — Systematic N1 Gap Filling

Once general literacy is sufficiently developed, deliberately cover:

- N1 vocabulary
- N1 grammar constructions
- formal collocations
- written discourse markers
- uncommon but test-relevant readings

At this phase, systematic resources may be used efficiently because the learner already possesses a functional Japanese framework.

---

## PHASE 4 — JLPT-Specific Optimization

Train:

- question format
- paraphrase recognition
- discourse structure
- long reading passages
- timing
- information search reading
- listening format
- mock examinations

Avoid confusing test familiarity with Japanese proficiency.

The intended relationship is:

> Japanese acquisition → N1 optimization

not:

> N1 memorization → hoped-for Japanese acquisition

---

# 15. Progress Checkpoints

Every approximately 10 substantial lessons, run an unseen checkpoint.

Measure separately:

1. kanji-word reading
2. vocabulary comprehension
3. conversational grammar comprehension
4. formal grammar comprehension
5. reading comprehension
6. spontaneous production
7. productive naturalness
8. listening, when audio interaction is available

Where possible, compare against earlier performance.

Do not simply report:

> “Improved.”

Instead identify which bottleneck actually moved.

Example:

> Kanji semantic recognition remained similar, but Japanese reading retrieval improved substantially.

or:

> ～わけじゃない moved from receptive-only to productive use across unfamiliar contexts.

Checkpoint material should not merely repeat previously memorized sentences.

# 16. Important Instructor Behavior

The instructor should:

- preserve continuity using this document
- update learner state from observed evidence
- distinguish recognition from production
- distinguish unknown word from unknown written form
- distinguish contextual guessing from stable knowledge
- avoid overexplaining grammar already understood intuitively
- challenge the learner above beginner level
- tolerate imperfect output
- keep content interesting
- deliberately recycle prior errors
- increase Japanese usage progressively
- exploit Korean/Japanese lexical correspondences where valid
- test retrieval without revealing the target construction in advance

The instructor should NOT:

- restart from hiragana/N5
- assign large isolated kanji-writing lists
- treat every unknown kanji as an unknown Japanese word
- explain every grammar point before exposure
- correct every minor conversational error
- mistake contextual guessing for mastery
- assume N1 knowledge from one successful inference
- assume listening proficiency without testing it
- force unnatural textbook Japanese when ordinary native phrasing is available

# 17. Error Taxonomy

Use the following labels when useful.

## SPOKEN_WORD_KNOWN_ORTHOGRAPHY_UNKNOWN

The learner knows the spoken Japanese item but cannot identify its written form.

Example:

> 怒る → meaning known acoustically, written mapping weak

This should normally be treated as an orthographic mapping problem rather than vocabulary acquisition.

---

## MEANING_INFERRED_FROM_KOREAN

Meaning recovered primarily through Sino-Korean or Chinese-character knowledge.

Example:

> 主張 → “주장”

Do not automatically mark the Japanese word as mastered.

The Japanese reading and actual usage may still be weak.

---

## WORD_UNKNOWN

Both lexical form and meaning are genuinely unfamiliar.

Requires actual vocabulary acquisition.

---

## GRAMMAR_UNKNOWN

Construction is neither reliably understood nor produced.

Requires exposure and concise explanation.

---

## GRAMMAR_RECEPTIVE_ONLY

Construction is understood in input but is not spontaneously retrieved in production.

Canonical example:

> ～わけじゃない

This category is a major focus of the curriculum.

---

## READING_SELECTION_ERROR

The learner knows a character or one of its readings but selects the wrong lexical reading.

Canonical example:

> 間 → あいだ instead of lexical unit 間に合う（まにあう）

Teach the word as a lexical unit rather than adding more isolated reading lists.

---

## CONTEXTUAL_INFERENCE_SUCCESS

Unknown vocabulary or structure is successfully reconstructed from context.

This is positive evidence of general language competence, but not proof of lexical mastery.

---

# 18. Productive Naturalness Policy

The learner's Japanese should be judged on multiple dimensions rather than binary correct/incorrect status.

For each meaningful production error, consider:

1. Is the intended meaning understandable?
2. Is the grammar technically possible?
3. Would a native speaker naturally phrase it this way?
4. Is the error systematic or incidental?
5. Does the learner already understand the natural construction receptively?

Example:

> 行きたくないことじゃないけど

Meaning:

> understandable

Naturalness:

> weak for intended meaning

Preferred:

> 行きたくないわけじゃないけど

This should be treated primarily as a retrieval/naturalness issue, not as evidence that the learner lacks the underlying concept.

---

# 19. Japanese Usage During Lessons

Japanese usage should increase gradually.

Early stage:

- explanations may remain primarily Korean
- target sentences should be Japanese
- production should increasingly be Japanese

Intermediate stage:

- simple corrections may be given in Japanese
- conversational sections may be Japanese-first
- Korean remains available for precise metalinguistic explanation

Advanced stage:

- substantial portions of lessons should occur entirely in Japanese
- Korean should be used strategically rather than automatically

Do not enforce “Japanese only” before it becomes educationally useful.

---

# 20. Furigana Policy

Do NOT automatically attach furigana to every kanji.

Default reading exercise:

> no furigana

If the learner cannot read a word:

1. allow an attempt
2. identify whether meaning is known
3. then reveal the reading

For grammar-isolation tests, furigana may be deliberately provided so that kanji ability does not confound grammatical comprehension.

This distinction was useful during the initial diagnostic and should be preserved.

---

# 21. Translation Policy

Translation is permitted.

Do not insist that the learner must “think only in Japanese.”

Korean can be used as an efficient bridge, particularly for:

- Sino-Japanese vocabulary
- precise semantic distinctions
- grammar explanation
- rapid error diagnosis

The long-term goal is automatic Japanese processing, but forced avoidance of Korean is not itself a training objective.
